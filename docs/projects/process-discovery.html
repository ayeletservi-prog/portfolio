Enterprise Web Verint Process Mining

Process Intelligence

Designed an AI-powered experience that automatically discovers business processes, recommends improvements, and enables ongoing monitoring of confirmed processes.

Overview

This project turned passive desktop observation into a trustworthy map of how work actually happens. Because automated detection is inherently noisy, the core of the design was a human review layer — giving analysts the tools to validate AI-discovered processes and their variants before any of it was trusted to drive downstream decisions.

The Problem

Documented processes rarely match how work actually happens. To improve a workflow — or even know it needs improving — an organization first needs an accurate, current picture of what employees are really doing, across teams and variations, without relying on interviews or diagrams that go stale the moment anything changes.

A bot addresses the first half of that problem by passively observing employee desktop activity and detecting recurring sequences of actions. Once a sequence has enough repeated observations for the bot to be confident it represents a genuine process, it's surfaced as a candidate. But automated detection is necessarily noisy: not every candidate is really one coherent process, and not every variant the bot groups under it actually belongs there. The system needed a human review layer an analyst could trust, before any of this data was used to drive decisions or optimizations.

01

Understanding the Needs

I started by mapping the full lifecycle a candidate process moves through — from raw, unvalidated observations, to a discovered process once the bot's confidence crosses a threshold, through analyst review, to a confirmed process that keeps accumulating data indefinitely. Designing for this meant designing for two different moments: an analyst doing first-pass triage on something new, and an analyst — or someone else entirely — later monitoring something already confirmed.

For the discovery phase specifically, I worked out what an analyst actually needs in order to triage a new candidate:

  • Is this really one coherent process? — an analyst needs to judge whether a bot-discovered candidate genuinely represents a real process, correctly described across all its variants.
  • Does every variant belong? — an analyst needs to confirm that every variant displayed for a process actually belongs to it.

That meant giving every discovered process three views at three altitudes — a flow diagram of the whole process, a breakdown of its individual variants, and the raw observation log behind all of it — so an analyst could move from the big picture down to hard evidence without ever leaving the page.

Once a process is confirmed, the needs shift to ongoing monitoring:

  • Is it still running correctly? — users need a continuous stream of data on a confirmed process to confirm it's performing as expected and catch any anomalies.
  • Can it be improved? — users need a way to check whether there's an opportunity to further optimize a confirmed process.
The design challenge

With no clearly defined requirements and a highly compressed timeline, the experience had to take shape while the product concept was still evolving. The challenge was to create a coherent end-to-end workflow while making deliberate trade-offs — focusing the MVP on the capabilities that delivered the most value and deferring less essential ideas for future phases.

02

Ideation & Design

Triage starts at the Discovered Processes table, where every candidate the bot has surfaced is listed side by side, before an analyst opens any single one of them.

Opening a process from that table drills into its dedicated view, made up of three tabs that each show the process from a different angle — starting with its general flow.

A user can confirm a process, or flag it — flagging sends it to the Flagged Processes table, where the services team takes the handling from there. The same principle applies to variants, which can be flagged the same way. I originally wanted users to be able to resolve these situations directly — for example, reassigning a variant that actually belonged to a different process, or marking one as not real so the bot could continue from there — but that wasn't technically feasible in the MVP's first phase, so I went with the simpler flagging flow instead.

Once a process is confirmed, it moves to the Confirmed Processes table, where its page gains two more tabs, turning it from a one-time triage screen into an ongoing monitoring tool: a Dashboard, and an AI variants optimization tab. The optimization tab proposes improved versions of specific variants as a current-vs-optimized comparison, with plain-language reasoning the analyst can accept or reject — and new suggestions surface proactively as a notification, rather than requiring anyone to go looking for them.

03

Outcome & Impact

Process Intelligence is not yet in general availability, but was unveiled at Verint Engage — the premier annual conference for customer experience and contact center professionals — where it generated significant interest from customers. The feature's first exposure to a real audience validated both the concept and the design direction.
